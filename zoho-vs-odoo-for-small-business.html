Zoho vs Odoo for Small Businesses: Which Should You Choose?

An honest comparison from a team that implements both.

Zoho vs Odoo

Why this comparison matters

Zoho and Odoo are two of the most popular business software suites for small and growing companies in India. Both cover CRM, sales, accounting, inventory, HR and more. Both can replace a tangle of spreadsheets. And both can fail if they are chosen for the wrong reasons.

We implement and customise both at Thryvmax, so this is not a sales pitch for either. It is a practical guide to how they differ and which kind of business each tends to suit.

The short answer

Choose Zoho if you want a broad set of ready-to-use cloud apps, strong CRM and sales tools, and a fast start with little technical overhead.

Choose Odoo if inventory, manufacturing or complex operations sit at the centre of your business, or if you want open-source software you can host and extend yourself.

If neither fits the way you work, a custom ERP or CRM may be the better option. We cover that in custom ERP vs off-the-shelf ERP.

What Zoho is

Zoho is a cloud software company headquartered in Chennai, India. Its suite includes Zoho CRM, Zoho Books, Zoho Inventory, Zoho People, Zoho Desk, Zoho Projects, Zoho Mail and Zoho Creator, a low-code platform for building custom apps. Zoho One bundles most of these apps under one subscription.

Zoho is fully cloud-hosted and sold as a subscription, usually priced per user. Customisation is done through settings, workflows, Zoho Creator, and Deluge, Zoho’s own scripting language.

What Odoo is

Odoo is an open-source business suite. It comes in two editions: Community, which is free and open source, and Enterprise, a paid edition with additional apps, features and support. Odoo can run on Odoo’s own cloud, on Odoo.sh, or on your own server.

Odoo is built in Python, and almost everything in it can be changed with custom modules. That makes it very flexible, especially for inventory, manufacturing and accounting-heavy businesses.

Side-by-side: how they differ

Hosting. Zoho is cloud-only. Odoo can be cloud-hosted or self-hosted, which gives you more control but also more responsibility for servers, backups and updates.

Getting started. Zoho apps are quick to switch on and configure. Odoo usually needs more planning and setup before go-live, particularly for inventory and manufacturing.

Customisation. Zoho is customised through configuration, Deluge scripts and Creator apps. Odoo is customised with Python modules, which allows deeper changes but needs developers who know Odoo.

CRM and sales. Zoho CRM is one of Zoho’s strongest products, with mature automation, pipelines and reporting. Odoo’s CRM is capable and tightly linked to its sales and inventory apps.

Inventory and manufacturing. This is where Odoo is usually stronger, with detailed warehouse, bill of materials and work order features. Zoho Inventory suits simpler trading and distribution needs well.

Cost model. Zoho is a per-user subscription. Odoo Community has no licence fee but still needs hosting and implementation; Odoo Enterprise adds a subscription. In both cases, implementation and customisation are usually a significant part of the total cost, so compare the full picture, not just licence prices.

Which businesses suit Zoho

In our experience, Zoho tends to suit service businesses, sales-led teams, education institutions, financial services firms and distributors who want sales, finance, support and email in one ecosystem without managing servers. It is also a strong choice when you want to start with one app, such as CRM, and grow into others. Read more in Zoho One for the service industry.

Which businesses suit Odoo

Odoo tends to suit manufacturers, traders with complex stock, and businesses whose operations need deep customisation, or who prefer open-source software they control. It also suits companies that want inventory, manufacturing, purchase and accounting tightly linked in one database.

Questions to ask before you decide

Which three processes cause the most pain today, and which platform handles them better out of the box? How many users will need access, now and in two years? Do you need to host data on your own servers, or is cloud fine? Who will maintain the system after go-live? And what does the total cost look like, including implementation, customisation, training and support?

Answering these honestly usually makes the choice clear. If it doesn’t, a short discovery conversation with someone who has implemented both will.
